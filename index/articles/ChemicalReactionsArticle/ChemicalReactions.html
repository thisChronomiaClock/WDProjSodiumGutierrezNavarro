Chemical Reactions

When you mix a bunch of things, you get a mixture. But sometimes, the properties of those things 'resonate' and interact with each other in a way that produces a new thing, or break things into new things. That is a chemical reaction. And they come in different structures and mechanisms.

Chemical Reactions: When things produce new things

A chemical reaction is a process where chemicals interact to form new chemicals with different compositions. The way the chemicals react is based on their properties. Usually we represent chemical reactions with chemical equations. We use an arrow to represent change:

A(aq) + B(g) -> C(s) + D(l)

The chemicals at the left of the arrow are called reactants. The stuff being chemically changed. The chemicals to the right of the arrow are our products. Basically the byproducts of our reactants mixing. The letters enclosed in parentheses tell you its state of matter. S for solid, L for liquid, G for gas, and AQ for aqueous.

An accurate chemical equation is usually written correctly and balanced. Meaning:

  • Chemicals are written with its chemical formula, and
  • the number of atoms of each element must be the same for both sides. This can be done by writing coefficients on the front of each chemical to match the number of atoms of each element on both sides.

An example is:

2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O

Notice how both sides of the equation have the same number of Hydrogen atoms (4) and Oxygen atoms (2).

Balancing Chemical equations

Now we show an example of getting a balanced chemical equation.

Ex. H2 + N2 -> NH3

We count the atoms of each element on each side:

2H 2N -> 3H 1N

Because there's one N atom on the product side and two on the reactant, we can even it out by putting a coefficient of 2 on NH3:

H2 + N2 -> 2NH3

Now there's:

2H 2N -> 6H 2N

Almost there! We can balance the electrons by putting a coefficient of 3 on H2. Because 3 * 2 = 6, therefore:

3H2 + N2 -> 2NH3

6H 2N -> 6H 2N

We have a balanced equation!

Chemical equations come in handy when we deal with HOW MUCH of a chemical has reacted or is needed to react. This branch of Chemistry is called Stoichiometry and will be introduced in the next article.

Types of Chemical Reactions

There are 5 main types of chemical reactions.

Synthesis

This is when two or more chemicals react together to form one new product. This is usually in the form of:

A + B -> AB

Decomposition

This is when one chemical 'dissolves' into multiple products and is in the form of:

AB -> A + B

Single Replacement

Say you have two chemicals. One of them is composed of a single atom (or multiple of the same element but it depends), and the other is a molecule made of 2 elements. The diatomic molecule will 'transfer' one of its ions to the single atom chemical Usually it will look like:

AB + C -> AC + B

Double Replacement

Imagine 2 molecules each composed of an anion and a cation. The molecules will 'exchange' cations or anions.

AB + CD -> AC + BD or AD + BC

Combustion

This is a special type of reaction. A substance reacts with O2, and produces energy in the form of light and heat. One of the products is H2O in the gaseous state, or vapor. A usual combustion reaction occurs with a hydrocarbon, a molecule made of only C and H atoms. In that case, the products are always carbon dioxide and water vapor.

There are special cases though, but we won't go too in detail.

Precipitation

This is a special type of reaction that produces an insoluble solid, known as the precipitate.

Acid Base/ Neutralization

This is a reaction when an acid and a base mix together. What an acid and base is won't be discussed now. All that you need to know is that the products are an ionic compound and water

Redox Reaction

This reaction involves the transfer of electrons, changing the oxidation numbers (or simply charge) of molecules and atoms.

When you model a chemical reaction with a chemical reaction, you will see how each molecule behaves, and this is important especially in manufacturing and other jobs that involve some chemical reactions in some way. Overall, chemical reactions are the ways to interact with a chemical, and keep the chemical domain moving.
