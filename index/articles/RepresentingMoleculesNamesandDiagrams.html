Representing Molecules: Names and Diagrams

Types of Molecules

Molecules are groups of atoms bonded together. They can be broadly classified into different types based on their composition and bonding:

  • Elemental Molecules – Made of only one type of atom (e.g., O₂, N₂).
  • Compound Molecules – Contain two or more different elements (e.g., H₂O, CO₂).
  • Diatomic Molecules – Consist of two atoms (e.g., H₂, Cl₂).
  • Polyatomic Molecules – Contain more than two atoms (e.g., NH₃, CH₄).

Molecular Bonds

Atoms in molecules are held together by chemical bonds. The main types include:

Ionic Bonds

Formed when electrons are transferred from one atom to another, creating oppositely charged ions that attract each other (e.g., NaCl).

Covalent Bonds

Formed when atoms share electrons. These bonds can be:

  • Single Bonds – One shared pair of electrons
  • Double Bonds – Two shared pairs
  • Triple Bonds – Three shared pairs

Polar vs Nonpolar Covalent Bonds

Covalent bonds can be polar (unequal sharing of electrons) or nonpolar (equal sharing), depending on the electronegativity of the atoms involved.

Representing Molecules with Diagrams

Lewis Structures

Lewis structures are diagrams that show the arrangement of valence electrons in a molecule. They help visualize bonding and lone pairs.

Key Features of Lewis Structures:

  • Dots represent valence electrons
  • Lines represent shared electron pairs (bonds)
  • Lone pairs are shown as pairs of dots

Example: Water (H₂O)

  ..
H-O-H
  ..

The oxygen atom has two lone pairs and forms two single bonds with hydrogen atoms.

Other Diagram Types

  • Structural Formulas – Show how atoms are connected
  • Ball-and-Stick Models – 3D representations of molecules
  • Space-Filling Models – Show relative atom sizes

Nomenclature: Naming Molecules

Ionic Compounds

Ionic compounds are named based on their ions:

  • Name the cation (metal) first
  • Name the anion (nonmetal) second, ending in -ide

Example: NaCl → Sodium Chloride

Transition Metals

Use Roman numerals to indicate the charge:

Example: FeCl₂ → Iron (II) Chloride

Covalent Compounds

Covalent compounds use prefixes to indicate the number of atoms:

  • Mono- (1)
  • Di- (2)
  • Tri- (3)
  • Tetra- (4)
  • Penta- (5)

Example: CO₂ → Carbon Dioxide

The first element does not use "mono-" if only one atom is present.

Hydrates

Hydrates are compounds that include water molecules. They are named by adding a prefix and the word hydrate.

Example: CuSO₄·5H₂O → Copper (II) Sulfate Pentahydrate

  • Mono- (1)
  • Di- (2)
  • Tri- (3)
  • Tetra- (4)
  • Penta- (5)

Understanding how molecules are represented through diagrams and names is essential in chemistry. It allows scientists to communicate structure, composition, and behavior clearly and accurately.
