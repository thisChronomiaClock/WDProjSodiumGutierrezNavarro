Stoichiometry

Introduction to Stoichiometry: Computing Interactions

Stoichiometry is the study of the quantitative relationships between reactants and products in a chemical reaction. It allows chemists to predict how much of a substance is needed or produced.

Balanced Chemical Equations

A balanced chemical equation shows the correct ratio of reactants and products. These coefficients are essential for stoichiometric calculations.

Example:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

This means:

  • 2 moles of hydrogen react with 1 mole of oxygen
  • to produce 2 moles of water

The Mole Concept

The mole is a unit used to count particles such as atoms and molecules. One mole contains Avogadro’s number of particles.

  • 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles
  • Molar mass (g/mol) allows conversion between grams and moles

Stoichiometric Calculations

Basic Steps

  1. Write and balance the equation
  2. Convert given quantity to moles
  3. Use mole ratio from the equation
  4. Convert to desired unit

Example Problem

How many grams of water are produced from 4 moles of hydrogen? 

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Mole ratio: 2 mol H₂ → 2 mol H₂O Therefore: 4 mol H₂ → 4 mol H₂O Convert to grams: 4 mol × 18 g/mol = 72 g H₂O

Limiting and Excess Reactants

In most reactions, one reactant is used up first. This is called the limiting reactant and determines the amount of product formed.

  • Limiting Reactant – Completely consumed
  • Excess Reactant – Remains after reaction

Percent Yield

Percent yield compares the actual yield (experimental result) to the theoretical yield (calculated result). 

Percent Yield = (Actual Yield / Theoretical Yield) × 100%

This helps evaluate the efficiency of a chemical reaction.

Types of Stoichiometry Problems

  • Mole-to-mole conversions
  • Mass-to-mass calculations
  • Limiting reactant problems
  • Gas stoichiometry (using gas laws)
  • Solution stoichiometry (using molarity)

Stoichiometry is a fundamental tool in chemistry that allows accurate prediction of chemical reactions. Mastery of this concept is essential for both laboratory work and theoretical analysis.
